# Interview: what to ask, in their words

Ask **one question per message**, in everyday words, and say in a few words
what the answer changes. The list below is a checklist, not a script.
- Replies can answer several items at once, in any order. Tick off
  everything they've given you and ask only for what's missing, roughly in
  this order.
- Never ask the same item more than twice.

## Checklist

1. **What the startup does.** "In one sentence, what does your startup do?
   Rough words are fine." This is a typed answer. Everything you write
   later must match it. If their story already makes it clear, read it back
   and confirm with a choice (Yes, that's it / Not quite).
2. **The point of the video.** "What should people understand or do after
   watching?" This decides the ending. If they're unsure, offer endings that
   don't assume facts. Ask, for example, whether the app can already be
   downloaded before offering "download".
3. **Where it's going.**
   - Instagram, Reels or Shorts: vertical. Just say so; no need to ask.
   - A website, YouTube or a pitch: wide.
   - Anything else: ask "Tall like a phone, or wide like a laptop?"
4. **Their story.** "Tell me the story the way you'd tell a friend: who has
   the problem, what goes wrong, how your app fixes it." Their phrases become
   the lines. Note their exact wording.
5. **The name.** The startup or app name, exactly as written. Never put a
   placeholder name into anything they will judge.
6. **The tagline** (next message): any line they always use, or "none".
7. **Who is in it.**
   - At most 2 characters.
   - For each one, reuse a saved character (list them by name) or make a new
     one. New ones go through the character steps in SKILL.md.
   - If the founder appears, ask whether that's them.
8. **Voices, one character per message.**
   - Their own recording, or a free AI voice?
   - For AI, let them hear before choosing. Pick 2 to 3 fitting voices from
     references/formats.md and run
     `voices.mjs STUDIO/voice-samples/<character id> "<that character's first line>" <v1> <v2> [<v3>]`.
     Open `samples.wav` (it plays them in order), then ask with options
     "Voice 1", "Voice 2", "Voice 3", each with one word for how it sounds,
     your pick first.
9. **The place.** Office, or a plain coloured studio (and which colour).
   For a fashion app the studio usually looks cleaner.
10. **Outfits in the story** (try-on videos).
    - Which outfits they want to show, in their words (colour, style,
      pattern).
    - Whether each change is a spin (twirl) or a sparkle swap.

## Example choices (question tool)

- **What the video is for:** Get downloads / Explain what it does / Grow the
  page / Show a new feature.
- **Format:** Tall for Reels and Shorts (Recommended for Instagram) / Wide for
  a website or YouTube.
- **Voices:** Free AI voice / My own voice / Mix.
- **AI voice:** each option names a voice and one word for how it sounds.
- **Place:** Plain studio in your brand colour / Office.
- **Twirl or swap:** Spin into it / Sparkle swap.
- **Character pick:** A / B / C, each with a few words on what's different.
- **STOPs:** Looks good / Change something.

## Phrases that work

- "Do you want A or B? I'd go with A, because ___."
- "That changes ___; everything else stays the same."
- "Here's a picture; tell me what you'd change, or say 'looks good'."

## Words to avoid with them

JSON, render, script file, folder path, frame rate, LUFS, Rhubarb, Kokoro,
resvg, ffmpeg, rig, pose, gesture names. Say instead: picture, video, the
written lines, smoothness, volume, lip-sync, AI voice, movement. Show a file
path only when they need to put a recording there.
